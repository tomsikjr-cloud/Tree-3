The rules of growth

1
Choose from three colors.

Every node is coral, blue, or gold. The letters A, B, C identify them too.

2
Stay within your allowance.

Tree 1 has one node. Tree 2 can have up to two. Each turn adds room for one more.

3
Never contain an earlier tree.

Colors and branching must match. An edge can stretch along a path. Left and right don’t matter.

FIELD GUIDE / 01

Make a tree. Break the pattern.

  1. Choose a color and click the canvas to create a root.
  2. Select a node, then click an empty spot to add a child. The + Child button automatically spaces branches apart. Drag nodes to rearrange them. The root is marked with a small ring.
  3. On turn n, submit a nonempty tree with at most n nodes. You may use fewer.
  4. You lose if any earlier tree embeds in your submission: colors match, ancestry and branching are preserved, and edges may stretch into paths. An earlier root may match a node anywhere in the new tree.
Example: A blue → gold chain is also found inside a blue → coral → gold chain. The extra coral node just stretches the edge. Two sibling branches, however, must stay in separate branches.

Position, edge length, and sibling order do not count. Crossing lines do not create connections. Editing is free; only Submit tree judges your move.

Select nodes with Tab. Enter selects a focused node. “+ Child” adds a child with the chosen color. Delete removes a selected branch. Ctrl/⌘ Z undoes an edit.

TREE(3) describes the longest possible valid sequence. Your score counts accepted trees in this run. Progress and your best score are saved in this browser when storage is available.
